# UI/UX audit and revision plan — 2026-09-26

Lens: the frontend-design skill (distinct, subject-grounded, no templated
defaults) inside the project's own rules in `docs/design-guidelines.md`
(which win where they speak: system-ui first, tabular numerals for
measurements, display tracking −0.02 em, 44 pt targets). Also folds in the
front-end findings of the 2026-09-26 honesty and security red teams.

## Brief, restated

- **Subject:** fitting a computer mouse to a hand, by measuring the hand on a
  sheet of paper. The world it borrows from: technical drawing, calipers,
  industrial-design sketches — not gaming RGB, not SaaS dashboards.
- **Audience:** someone about to buy a mouse, on their phone.
- **Primary job of the landing:** get them to "Scan my hand".

## Findings

### Landing (`/`)

1. Tracked all-caps eyebrow joined with middle dots ("EARLY PREVIEW ·
   HAND-FIT RANKING") — a template tell and an unnecessary label. → Remove.
   Keep the honest preview status as one sentence-case line under the CTA:
   "Early preview — measurements are still being validated."
2. "01 / 02 / 03" on three statements that are not a sequence. → Drop the
   numbers; a quiet list of three statements with a bold lead-in each. Keep
   the hairlines only if they separate; otherwise space alone.
3. Four identical rounded spec cards under the sketch — the SaaS card kit.
   → Make the specs part of the drawing: one dimension line under the sketch
   ("125 mm" centred on a thin line with end ticks) and a compact
   annotation row set in tabular figures: `125 × 63.5 × 40 mm   60 g`
   (× is real dimension notation). No cards.
4. "How it works →" — appended arrow. → "How it works", text link.
5. Copy: "Shape matters more than specs." sits directly above a spec strip —
   it argues against the page's own content. → Headline "Measure your hand.
   Find the mouse that fits." Subhead "A blank sheet of A4 and your phone are
   all it takes."
6. The one bold element is the sketch. Give it room (full content width,
   ~300 px tall), keep everything else quiet.

### How it works (`/how-it-works`)

7. The illustration shows the A4 **landscape**; the real scan uses it
   **portrait** (Kirby). → Use `public/images/hand-on-a4-camera.png` (portrait
   paper), cropped to a 342 × 480 card, the four checks placed on the paper's
   corners (pen.dev frame 18 shows the positions), matching
   `screens/18-how-it-works.png`.
8. "Step by step" pill above the title — an unnecessary label. → Remove.
9. The three steps _are_ a sequence → number them 1 · 2 · 3 in the step
   markers (replace the icons with the numerals, same tile).

### Consistency and copy (all screens)

10. One action name for starting a scan: **"Scan my hand"** everywhere
    (login "Start measuring without signing in" → "Scan my hand";
    "Scan without an account" → "Scan without an account" stays, it's a
    different action).
11. The privacy promise is phrased three ways (home, `/scan`, camera). → One
    exported constant: "Your photo never leaves your phone. Only measurements
    are sent." Reuse it everywhere; the camera's shorter line may keep "The
    camera view stays on your phone." as a second, distinct sentence.

### Results

12. Left-handed users: until M1 passes, no mouse carries handedness data, so
    nothing is ever excluded as `wrong_hand`, silently. → When the scan's hand
    is left, show one line near the scores: "Left-hand fit isn't rated yet —
    check each mouse's shape before you buy."
13. When every mouse fits poorly (top total below a candidate threshold of
    50), the top pick is styled like a confident recommendation. → Show one
    honest line above it: "None of these fits your hand well. The closest is
    below." Threshold in one constant, marked candidate.
14. Heading hierarchy: ~29 parallel `<h3>` in the ranked list with no `<h2>`
    parent. → Give the list an `<h2>` ("Other matches").

### Camera (live capture)

15. In printed-sheet mode the live cues still say "paper". → Cue nouns follow
    `calibrationMode` ("sheet" vs "paper").
16. Security M1: the permission prompt can resolve after unmount, leaving
    the camera on. → Track mount/abort; if the stream arrives after unmount
    or outside the "requesting" state, stop its tracks immediately; also stop
    on `visibilitychange` while requesting.
17. Security L5: revoke the review `previewUrl` on unmount.
18. The old camera spec (`docs/design/camera-capture-2026-09-25/README.md`)
    still describes a live bank-card outline that was dropped with plain
    paper. → Mark those lines superseded.
